A large share of fluent language is prefabricated: chunks, collocations, sentence frames. The disagreement is about **what follows for teaching**: notice chunks as they emerge (Thornbury), pre-select and flood them (Conti), or treat them as the whole syllabus (Lewis).

## Where the sources disagree
- **Selection:** Lewis gives no syllabus. Conti selects by communicative function → pattern. Thornbury combines a frequent-formulae core with emergent items.
- **Fluency mechanism:** chunk *storage* (Pawley & Syder, Thornbury) vs process *automatisation* (Goodey's comment, DeKeyser via Conti). These are complementary. Chunks are what gets automatised. *(Claude)*
- **Risk:** "all chunks, no pineapple": retrievable formulae without generative grammar.
- **Bottom-up grammar:** Dellar and Selivan say most "grammar" errors are colligation/priming gaps; Thornbury (Error) and Pienemann via Conti say many are developmental. Both can be true: word-pattern errors (*I am agree*) look lexical, question-order errors look developmental. *(Claude)*
- **Chunks → rules:** Selivan (citing N. Ellis 1996) says chunks bootstrap grammar; Walter (2012) says chunk teaching avoids grammar. Conti sequences both (chunks first, rules in Expansion).
