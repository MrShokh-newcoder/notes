Listening is the skill most often *tested* rather than *taught*. Sources agree on combining bottom-up decoding (segmentation, connected speech) with top-down and metacognitive work, and on listening as input for learning, not only as an exercise.

## Where the sources disagree
- **How central is comprehensible input?** Smith: "the main driver". Weller: "necessary but not sufficient" (Swain's output, Schmidt's noticing). Conti: structured, flooded input followed by structured output.
- **How much decoding?** Field/Smith and Conti put decoding and word recognition first; Smith still calls communicative interaction "the go-to way". Transcription is "dull" for teens.
- **Silent period:** Harding da Rosa treats Krashen's silent period as fact; SLA research shows it varies widely between learners *(Claude)*.
