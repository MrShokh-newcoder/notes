Learners acquire grammar along developmental routes that instruction can speed up but not reorder. The practical question is how to reconcile a coursebook's grammar sequence with the learner's internal syllabus.

## Where the sources disagree
- **Emergence vs pre-selection:** Thornbury lets grammar emerge and reacts. Conti pre-selects patterns and explains grammar only *after* processing. Both reject PPP's "present, then produce".
- **Readiness:** Conti (via Pienemann) says you cannot quiz a learner into the next stage. Emergentists say stages are themselves frequency effects.
- Dellar attacks PPP and tense syllabuses but concedes a place for traditional form–meaning work. Swift's Module 1 methodology answers treat guided discovery and structural approaches as positions to *explain* (beliefs, roles, suitability), not to refute. In the exam, describe the debate; don't take a side.
