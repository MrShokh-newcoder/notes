Errors are evidence of an interlanguage system, not only failures. Respond according to their *source* (transfer / developmental / teaching-induced) and whether they impede communication.

## Where the sources disagree
- **Transfer vs developmental:** Thornbury doubts that most errors are L1 interference. Teachers in single-L1 classes (Jaworski) see transfer everywhere.
- **Written CF:** contested (Truscott vs Ferris). See [[ELT Research Bites - reading queue]].
- **Synthesis across sources *(Claude)*:** Lyster & Ranta (1997) found recasts rarely produce uptake, while prompts (elicitation, metalinguistic cues) do. Tsateri says connect first, correct later (on intuition). Ellis & Shintani (via Fluency First) say both immediate and delayed correction can help. The best-supported principle is to be **selective** (few, global, developmental errors) and **noticeable**, more than any rule about timing.
- **In-flow vs delayed:** Underhill (Demand High) upgrades language *during* fluency work with silent cues; commenters and the delayed-feedback tradition say interrupting fluency harms it, especially with anxious learners.
- **Prevention vs correction:** Dellar: "prevention is infinitely desirable to cure" (design tasks within learners' priming). Pinard: a structured response sequence with explicit, varied techniques. These are complementary: Dellar is about planning, Pinard about the moment.
