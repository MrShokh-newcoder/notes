> Points, levels, badges, lives and leaderboards in learning apps run on the same learner data as adaptive systems. The motivation they create is extrinsic and short-lived: learners find shortcuts, interest drops, and novelty fades once the mechanics become standard.

## Claim
"Gamification and adaptive learning go together like hand in glove because of the data that is generated." Selwyn (2013, *Distrusting Educational Technology*, p. 101) calls it **pointsification**.

## Design implications
- Tie points to learning actions that matter (retrieval, production), not completion.
- Expect novelty decay: vary rewards and tasks; avoid a single points leaderboard.
- Check for shortcut behaviour. Points that can be gamed don't track learning.
- Make social or cooperative play central. Leaderboards are "solitary" (Clandfield, comment).
- Gamification is a layer inside a learning design, not the design.

## Limits
A 2014 opinion post with little research cited. For the evidence, see Dehghanzadeh et al. (2019) systematic review in [[Vocabulary apps - reward design outruns learning design]].

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- These are the same conclusions as [[Rewards undermine interest in the rewarded activity]], reached from the edtech side. For the RS Learning System, award points for *delayed retrieval and production*, never for logins or minutes.
