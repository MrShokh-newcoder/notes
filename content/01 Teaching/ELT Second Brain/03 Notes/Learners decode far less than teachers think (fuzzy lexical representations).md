> Simpson's Oxford PhD (2024): ordinary pupils badly misunderstand simple sentences through segmentation errors, fuzzy word representations, L1 interference and missed grammatical markers. Teachers overestimate what beginners can decode.

## Study
Simpson, K. (2024). *The Developmental Trajectory of Second Language Listening Errors*. DPhil thesis, University of Oxford. ([ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:fd5c080b-850d-4276-8ead-3c6f5dada9cc))
- Examples: *bouteille d'eau* heard as "potato" by a third of participants; *sa sœur Martine est très travailleuse* → "Martin has nice eyes".
- "Self-subtitling": learners mentally transcribe words they saw on earlier slides.
- Strategy use develops more in older learners. "Vocabulary trumps grammar."

## Theory
Fuzzy Lexical Representation Hypothesis (the post cites "Gor et al." with conflicting years, 2010 and 2021; check before citing). Complex dynamic systems; Field's micro-skills.

## Smith's takeaways
Micro-skill tasks (phoneme games, word-boundary spotting); teach words *through listening* and insist on accurate pronunciation to sharpen representations; intensive, scaffolded, low-stakes listening; "an overhaul of the expectations teachers have of the purpose of listening in class" (Simpson).

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Words learned from print only (flashcards, wordlists) give fuzzy *sound* representations. That's a mechanism-level reason why Movers/Flyers learners "know" words they can't hear. Always teach new words aurally first.
