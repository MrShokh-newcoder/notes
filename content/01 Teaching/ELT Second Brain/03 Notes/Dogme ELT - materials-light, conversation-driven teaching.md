> Dogme: learning is socially motivated and socially constructed, so teach with the language that emerges from the needs, interests and desires of the people in the room.

## Claim
History and closure notice for the Dogme ELT Yahoo list (started 9 March 2000, an idea of David French; about 15,000 posts in 10 years). Core tenets from the masthead: materials-light; language learning is "socially motivated and socially constructed"; content comes from the people in the room. Book form: *Teaching Unplugged* (Meddings & Thornbury 2009). The name is an analogy with the Dogme 95 film rules.

## Mechanism / theory
Social-constructivist (implicit, uncited in the post). Motivation argument: students *want* to speak because the content is theirs.

## Classroom moves
Conversation-led lessons; attend to emergent language (see [[The 5 Rs of reactive teaching]]); learners' lives supply content.

## Limits & critique
- Self-referential; makes no empirical claims.
- Hard for inexperienced teachers (comment); the list felt like an "in crowd" (Renshaw).
- Thornbury retracts a misrepresentation of Sylvia Ashton-Warner in his exchange with Harmer (the "book-burning" charge).

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Full Dogme clashes with Prepare 2e plus a school-wide coursebook ladder. Use it as a **slot** instead: a 10–15 min unplugged opener or a weekly "unplugged" lesson. Your Wednesday speaking challenge group is a natural home for it.
- YL boundary: very young learners lack the L2 resources for conversation-driven lessons. Stories and routines do the "learner-centred content" job there (see [[Stories as shared contexts for language]]).
