> Practice tests teach format and timing, but their learning value comes from structured reflection afterwards: one strength, one or two targets, a test-taker's journal, and talking strategies aloud.

## Routine
- After each mock: one strength + one to two areas to improve; compare with a partner.
- **Test-taker's journal** headings: Strategies · Time management · Goals; sentence stems for anxiety.
- Groups apply the official criteria to a sample answer under a time limit, then rotate.
- A short calming routine before the test.

## Limits
TOEFL-specific materials; tips-style, unreferenced.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Same journal for IELTS and Cambridge mocks; add a column for *error type → cause → fix* (knowledge, strategy, time, misreading). That's what makes the mock diagnostic.
