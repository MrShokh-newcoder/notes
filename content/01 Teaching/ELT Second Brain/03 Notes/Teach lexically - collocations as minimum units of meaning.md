> 'Collocations, and not individual words, are minimum units of meaning.' Four small shifts: ban single words, explore more and explain less, avoid word-for-word L1 equivalence, and revisit 'known' words through new partners.

## Four principles
1. **Ban single words.** Board new words with their partners (*do homework*, *heavy rain*, *investigate the murder (of)*), minimum two words.
2. **Explain less, explore more.** Examples and concordance lines over definitions, which prompts "What else can *mild* go with?"
3. **No word-for-word equivalence.** Translate whole phrases, not words.
4. **Attend to "known" words.** Familiar words combine into unfamiliar meanings (*lose your cool*, *make ends meet*).

## Theory
"Teaching lexically" is Dellar & Walkley's coinage. Distinguishes compositional from non-compositional collocations (*make do*). No research cited in the post.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Easy rule for all Rahimov teachers (could be a checklist item): *no single word on the board without a partner.* It's observable, cheap, and changes vocabulary notebooks immediately.
- Uzbek/Russian learners often translate word-for-word. Principle 3 targets the most common source of unnatural IELTS collocations (e.g. "make a photo" ← Russian *сделать фото*).
