> Spaced-repetition apps (Anki, Quizlet) beat paper cards but share their limits: learners rarely self-study, cards hold too little (collocation, usage), they build mainly receptive knowledge, and ready-made sets fit badly. Spacing isn't enough on its own; add generation, elaboration and better feedback.

## Part 1 ([2014-10-13](https://adaptivelearninginelt.wordpress.com/2014/10/13/spaced-repetition-and-the-classroom-part-1/))
Deliberate vocabulary learning is justified (Nation 2008), but cards are an "initial stage"; class time is needed for productive use. Generative use helps retention (Joe 1998).

## Part 2: three levers (Brown, Roediger & McDaniel 2014, *Make It Stick*)
- **Generation:** partial cues (*foot – s _ _ e*) beat showing the answer.
- **Elaboration:** connect to prior knowledge, multiple contexts.
- **Feedback:** staged hints; slightly delayed rather than instant.
- Classroom: pair testing, improving cards together, word circles, true/false personal sentences, mini-stories with target words (Sökmen 1997).

## Product design *(Claude, from Kerr)*
Card fields for collocation, example and usage; learner-authored cards; production prompts; staged hints; several contexts per item; A/B-test feedback timing.
