> MCQs, translations and mini-whiteboard checks show performance in that task, not acquisition. Knowing a rule, recognising it, processing it for meaning and using it spontaneously are four different achievements.

## Claim
"A correct answer in a controlled task is evidence of successful performance in THAT task, not proof that the construction has been acquired." Teachers get "systematically reassured by the wrong kinds of success". Result: weak listening and spontaneous production that only shows up at high-stakes exams.

## Mechanism / theory
Automaticity and practice (DeKeyser & Criado 2013; DeKeyser 2013); input processing and structured input (VanPatten 1996, 2004); interleaving and delayed retention (Nakata & Suzuki 2019); corrective feedback (Lyster & Saito 2010); Teachability (Pienemann 1989, 1998).

## Five low-prep checks that probe acquisition
1. **Listen and choose**: form–meaning in connected speech (A/B on whiteboards), then with temporal clues removed.
2. **Complete and transform**: stem → change the reason → remove the stem.
3. **Say something about yourself**: no model; does the form appear unprompted?
4. **Timed sentence challenge**: 5 seconds; measures retrieval efficiency.
5. **The unexpected comeback**: revisit the form 3 weeks later through a different topic, without naming it.

## Limits & critique
- Polemical tone; targets a named blogger (Bastow).
- Speeded *written* retrieval ≠ oral automaticity (he concedes).
- Conceptual. His checks aren't shown to predict outcomes either.
