> Adding affectively engaging questions and a personal project to a coursebook reading made an A1 class more invested. The idea comes from NILE's materials-development work on affective learning (and a Diaz Maggioli IATEFL plenary).

## Sequence (A1, small group)
1. Warm-up: compare where you live now with home (photos, feelings).
2. Reading with bilingual dictionaries, not translation apps.
3. Comprehension questions + affective ones ("Is it a good idea to build with glass and plastic? Why (not)?").
4. Describe the images; choose a favourite and justify it.
5. Project: draw a building you'd like to live in (materials, size, layout) and present it. Drawing skill not judged.

## Limits
Informal; the effect isn't separated from the project.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Easy upgrade for Kid's Box and Prepare readings: add one "how do you feel / would you…?" question and one personal mini-project per unit. It's the affective counterpart to [[Output tasks after reading boost vocabulary retention]].
