> Analysing the Elements of AI MOOC's peer review shows common design faults: ratings with no comment box, easy scales, incentives that reward speed, weak control of junk answers, and staff grading that makes the peer stage feel pointless.

## Design rules
- Give reviewers a **sample answer** and a short, specific rubric (e.g. on topic · complete · sound arguments · easy to understand).
- Add an **open justification** box, at least for low ratings.
- Monitor for copied or junk answers, and provide a report route.
- If you reward reviewing more peers, check that it doesn't reward speed over quality.
- Be clear whether staff checks affect the result.

## Related: reusable audio feedback ([2022](https://afteroctopus.wordpress.com/2022/06/08/on-talking-to-your-online-students/))
Short, group-neutral audio files ("done well" / "watch out") reusable across terms, always with a **transcript**. Captions aren't enough for screen readers.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Directly relevant to peer review in the RS Learning System: require a written reason for every score, and show reviewers an anchor example first. That's what makes peer assessment reliable (calibration).
