> ELF research suggests native-like accuracy isn't necessary and intelligibility is the realistic target. But accent bias persists, so there's no win-win: lay out the options and let learners choose.

## Points
- "Threshold level" of pronunciation for intelligibility (Celce-Murcia 1996).
- Accent bias: Hide & Poel (2000) report foreign-accented speakers judged as less intelligent by staff.
- The author's own shift from hiding a Russian-sounding accent to accepting it.

## Classroom (derived)
Discuss accent judgements → introduce ELF and intelligibility → learners choose a goal (intelligible vs native-like) and write it down (B2+) → align pronunciation work to it → revisit.

## Limits
Values-based; few concrete activities; little for learners who need a specific accent.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- IELTS Pronunciation descriptors reward intelligibility and the *range of features*, not accent. Teaching this explicitly reduces anxiety and redirects practice to stress, chunking and connected speech.
