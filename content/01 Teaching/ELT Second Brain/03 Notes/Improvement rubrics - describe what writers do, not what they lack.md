> A high-school department scores the same timed writing task in autumn and spring on a 12-strand 'improvement rubric' that places students on a continuum instead of labelling them 'far below basic'. Norming and trend charts turn scores into next steps.

## Process (grades 9–12)
1. Students respond to a reading passage (UC AWPE prompts), autumn and spring.
2. Teachers norm on high/medium/low papers over two days.
3. Score on 12 strands (ISAW programme), as positions on a continuum; explicitly not summative.
4. Chart patterns by grade; pick focus strands per unit.
5. Spring: re-score in a different colour to see which strands moved; set next year's goals.

## Limits
"A tremendous amount of work"; no outcome data; the rubric is only shown as an image.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Same design with Cambridge/IELTS writing criteria: a September and a May sample on the same prompt type, scored per criterion, gives a growth picture that a band score hides. Good evidence for parents and for DELTA Module 3 evaluation.
