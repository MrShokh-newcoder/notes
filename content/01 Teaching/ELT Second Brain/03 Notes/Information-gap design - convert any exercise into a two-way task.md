> Almost any closed exercise becomes a speaking task if you split the information between A and B so that neither can finish alone. The gap forces clear communication and gets reticent groups talking.

## Conversions
- **Two-version gap-fill:** same text, different words removed for A and B; partners dictate the missing words to each other.
- **Picture memory:** A and B study different pictures for 30 seconds, close books, describe from memory, then find similarities and differences.
- **Key swap:** after two worksheets, pairs exchange answer keys and check each other; the teacher only monitors.
- **Half-a-crossword:** A has half the answers, B the other half; they write and give clues (a [generator](https://github.com/monolithpl/half-a-crossword) exists).
- **Describe and draw** a famous painting, then exhibit the drawings.

## Why it works *(Claude)*
A required information exchange is one of Ellis's task criteria (a gap plus an outcome) and produces negotiation of meaning (Long's Interaction Hypothesis). Two-way gaps generate more negotiation than one-way ones (Pica et al. 1993). The conversions above are "task-like" practice rather than full tasks because the language is pre-set.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- KET/PET Reading Part 1 or Use of English items as two-version gap-fills: same exam content, ten minutes of speaking for free.
