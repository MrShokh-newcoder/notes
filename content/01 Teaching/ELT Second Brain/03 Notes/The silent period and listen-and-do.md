> Most young learners understand far more than they produce. Don't force output; 'responsive behaviour should be accepted as evidence that learning is taking place'. Listen-and-do tasks give them a safe channel.

## Activity ladder (6–7-year-olds)
- **Listen and do**: instructions + gesture → gradually drop the gesture. (A companion page pairs classroom verbs with signs, [Classroom Instructions](https://jmhdr.wordpress.com/classroom-instructions/).)
- **Listen and identify / respond**: True/False game ("The sky is green"), hold up or run to a card.
- **TPR** with chants and rhymes.
- **Listen and colour/draw/make**: "Picasso dictation"; a short colouring song caps colouring time.
- **Stories**: structure-based ("Bob! Where's Bob?"), vocabulary-based, context-based (a known story).
- Pacing heuristic: ~1 minute per year of age per activity (≈6–7 min max).

## Theory & limits
Krashen's silent period, presented as fact. In SLA research it's variable and not universal *(Claude)*. TPR (Asher, uncited). Wright, *Creating Stories with Children* (OUP).

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- These are exactly the Starters/Movers Listening Part formats (listen and colour, listen and draw lines). A silent-period-friendly lesson *is* exam preparation at this level.
