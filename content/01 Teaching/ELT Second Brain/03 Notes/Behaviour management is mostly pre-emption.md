> If lessons are well planned, varied and purposeful, and the teacher is clear and consistent, most problems never start. 25 strategies, centred on transitions, signals and catching children being good.

## Key strategies (selected from 25)
- **Transitions**: starts, ends and changes of activity are where trouble happens. Plan them as stages.
- **Attention signal**: one consistent gesture, tambourine or bell.
- **The waiting game**: open body language, neutral face, wait.
- **CBG: Catch them Being Good**. Praise on-task behaviour rather than constantly reprimanding.
- **Stir vs settle** activities (Halliwell 1992). Sequence lessons to manage energy.
- Vary seating and pairing criteria; demonstrate instead of explaining; a class behaviour contract, kept to.
- "Arc of scan": keep the whole room in view.

## Limits
"There are no easy answers, many complex variables." General rules, no age or class-size boundaries.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Stir/settle is the most transferable idea for 45-minute primary lessons in Tashkent. Audit Kid's Box lessons for back-to-back "stirrers" (songs, TPR then pairwork) and insert a settler (colouring dictation, quiet picture-word matching).
- Conduct scoring link: log *transition* behaviour separately in Axloqiy baholash data. It will probably show that most incidents cluster there.
