> The 'goldfish attention span' claim is unsupported: capacity on attention tests looks stable. What may have changed is willingness to invest effort, given digital habits and expectations of instant reward. Focus can be trained, and the environment matters.

## Evidence cited
- Attention tests (d2) show stable performance; lab vigilance drops after ~10 minutes.
- 2025 meta-analysis (*Computers in Human Behavior*): phones in class had a large negative effect on recall.
- Visual noise and classroom chatter impair learning (2021, 2022 studies).
- Interruptions longer than 15 s raise error rates (2017 review).
- Adolescent non-school screen time rose from 3.8 to 7.7 h/day during the pandemic (JAMA Pediatrics 2021).

## Moves
- Teach students how attention works (e.g. a selective-attention demonstration).
- Short movement or calm breaks at natural points.
- Protect sustained reading and writing blocks and lengthen them gradually.
- Phones out of reach; less noise and visual clutter.
- Connect hard tasks to meaningful goals.

## Limits
- US K-12 framing; the citations are as printed in the article and weren't checked against the primary papers.
- The phone–attention causal link is "plausible", not proven (the article's own word).
