> A worked example of Long's (2015) TBLT: a target task found by needs analysis ("dealing with problems in flight and suggesting actions") organises four lessons, and form work is chosen by what the task demands, not by a grammar sequence.

## Sequence (B2 aviation students, 4 x 45 min)
1. Live ATC clip with transcript: accents and gist.
2. Pairs sort utterances into **ATC phraseology** vs **plain English**.
3. ICAO texts on phraseology; true/false.
4. Focus on contractions and connected speech in extracts.
5. Micro-listening for function words and standard chunks.
6. Discuss intelligibility, then **exit tasks**: ATC role-play and a ~250-word essay, both pass/fail against ICAO Level 4.
- Focus-on-form list: homophones (*no/know*, *to/too/two*), ambiguity, modals, ELF accommodation strategies.

## Why it's useful
Most TBLT examples are general English. This shows the full Long logic: needs analysis → target task → pedagogic tasks → task-based assessment.

## Limits
One vocational group; no outcome data.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Exam classes have a ready-made needs analysis: the exam tasks *are* the target tasks. "Describe a photo and speculate" (PET Speaking Part 2) can be sequenced exactly like this, with form work chosen from what learners failed to say.
