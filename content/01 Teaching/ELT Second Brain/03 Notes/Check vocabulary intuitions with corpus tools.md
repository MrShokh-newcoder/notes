> Teachers' intuitions about near-synonyms (*trouble* vs *problem*) are often vague or wrong. Corpus tools give checkable evidence on collocation, frequency and patterns. 'You shall know a word by the company it keeps' (Firth 1957).

## Procedure
1. Write down your intuition first (he asked four teachers).
2. Check collocates and patterns: Sketch Engine's *Sketch Difference*; **COCA** (collocates within ±4 words).
3. Compare learner-dictionary entries (Cambridge).
4. Triangulate before concluding. Mutual Information (MI) > 3 usually signals a meaningful collocation.

## Theory
Firth (1957); Sinclair (1991) *Corpus, Concordance, Collocation*; O'Keeffe, McCarthy & Carter (2007) *From Corpus to Classroom*.

## Limits
Results depend on the corpus (BNC vs COCA = British vs American English). Tools change; some links are dead.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- IELTS learners ask "Can I say *make a decision* / *do a decision*?" daily. Teach them to check it themselves in a free corpus or SkELL. That's learner autonomy plus lexis.
- For DELTA M1 language analysis, corpus evidence makes "use" answers more precise.
