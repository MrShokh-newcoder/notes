> Teacher growth is non-linear and peer-dependent. Coaching develops one specific skill or behaviour and doesn't require subject expertise. Mentoring is long-term, spans several areas, and needs an expert. 'Trust is cultivated, it's demonstrated and it's prolonged once it's been earned.'

## Key points (IATEFL 2023 plenary, two parts)
- Four qualities to cultivate in colleagues: **trust, confidence, courage, perseverance** ([part 2](https://tdsig.org/2025/03/lean-on-me-stories-of-coaching-mentoring-and-teacher-resilience-divya-madhavan/)).
- Resilience is collective: "Our strength as a community has always been in the collective space."
- Managers must create time and recognition for coaching and mentoring; new habits need time (citing Clear, *Atomic Habits*).
- Critical-pedagogy lens (Freire; Giroux): who gets support in your school, and who doesn't?

## Limits
A narrative speech; the interviewees were chosen by the author; no burnout model or measurement.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Use the coaching/mentoring distinction in the checklist: new teachers get a *mentor* (a subject expert); experienced teachers working on one habit (e.g. wait time) get a *coach* (a peer is fine).
- The ustādh–shāgird tradition is mentoring in this sense: long-term, multi-area, trust-based.
