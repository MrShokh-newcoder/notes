> A song gap-fill lets students get by listening only for the gapped word. Stack tasks with different demands instead: learners write their own 'upgraded' version of a line, then transcribe the real line and compare.

## Sequence (B2–C1 teens, 60 min, "Thriller")
1. Warm-up: do you like horror stories? Why?
2. Elicit synonyms for *bad* and *waiting* to build the first line of a class horror story; play the song's first line; students transcribe; compare versions.
3. Pairs upgrade the next four lines, then transcribe the real lyrics and compare.
4. Chorus: find and correct one planted error per line.
5. Gap-fill of 1–4 words only at **squeeze zones** (fast, reduced speech), peer check, listen again.
6. Homework: guess underlined words from context, check, write definitions.

## Why it works *(Claude)*
Predict–transcribe–compare creates a noticing-the-gap moment between learner language and the model. Gapping only the squeeze zones targets decoding of connected speech, the real bottom-up difficulty (Field).
