> The 'global competitiveness' rationale is a poor basis for education. Rankings carry little useful information and turn learning into a zero-sum race. Aim for 'learning for all'.

## Claim
Judge progress in absolute terms, not by rank; design for collaboration; reject "tougher standards" that create artificial scarcity. "Our loyalty, after all, is not to corporations but to children."

## Limits
About national competitiveness, not classroom competition. His classroom-level essays ("The Case Against Competition", 1987; "Is Competition Ever Appropriate in a Cooperative Classroom?", 1993) are on the site but not yet processed.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Leaderboards are the gamification feature with the weakest evidence for learning and the clearest risk for low-ranked children. Prefer **personal-best** tracking and **team** goals (cooperative structures: Johnson & Johnson's research supports these).
