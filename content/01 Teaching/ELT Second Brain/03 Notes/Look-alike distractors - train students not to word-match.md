> In multiple-choice reading, 'hares' pick the option that repeats words from the text; 'tortoises' read for meaning and avoid the look-alike distractor, even when they're weaker readers. Teach the trap explicitly.

## Procedure
1. Short video lesson on the "look-alike trap".
2. Worksheet: eight exam items, each with the wrong answer an "unknown student" chose. Two versions: critical words underlined, or no hints.
3. Teacher glosses vocabulary; students explain *why* each chosen option is wrong.
4. A discussion tool, not self-study.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Cambridge and IELTS writers build exactly these distractors (word-matched options; IELTS T/F/NG where the words match but the claim differs). Use the "analyse the wrong answer someone chose" format with past-paper items: it turns error analysis into a reading strategy lesson.
- Works for KET Reading Part 3/4, PET Part 3/5 and IELTS Reading.
