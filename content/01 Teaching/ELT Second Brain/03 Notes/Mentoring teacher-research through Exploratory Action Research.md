> Exploratory Action Research (EAR) is designed 'for busy teachers in quite difficult circumstances': explore a classroom problem with light data, then plan, act and evaluate. Mentors guide with questions, not solutions.

## The book reviewed
*Stories of Mentoring Teacher-Research* (eds. Smith, Eraldemir Tuyan, Serra, Békés; IATEFL ReSIG and MenTRnet, 2024, ISBN 978-1-912588-50-3). 20 mentor narratives from ~22 regions, **including Uzbekistan**.

## EAR
Smith & Rebolledo (2018) *A Handbook for Exploratory Action Research* (British Council, free). Phases: *explore* (understand the problem before acting) → *act* → *evaluate*.

## Mentoring moves
- Don't solve the problem. Ask the right question at the right time: "mentees need guidance and inspiration, not direct solutions."
- Time is the main constraint, so schedule regular check-ins.
- Move from external blame (students, coursebook, timetable) to achievable changes.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- EAR is a natural bridge to DELTA Module 2 experimental practice and the PDA. A Rahimov "teacher-research circle" could use the free British Council handbook.
