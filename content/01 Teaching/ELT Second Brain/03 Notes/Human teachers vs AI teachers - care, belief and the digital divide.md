> 'Human or AI teacher' is the wrong question. Humans offer care, shared experience and belief in learners, which AI only simulates. The real need is teachers who can work with AI. Expect AI tutors to feel more human, and the digital divide to widen.

## Points
- Mitra's "Hole in the Wall" (1999) is cited as evidence of self-directed learning with technology *(Claude: its findings and interpretation are contested)*.
- A "supportive friend" prompt: the bot replies to a learner's news with brief encouragement and no explanations.
- Equity: learners without good devices or connectivity fall further behind.

## Limits
- Each post ends by promoting the author's paid books or courses; no evidence of learning outcomes.
- Speculative; "AI will never…" is an assertion.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- The homeschool-platform pitch should lead with **teacher/parent + AI**, not AI replacing them. That's the more credible claim (Bloom's 2-sigma tutoring result came from human tutors, not machines).
