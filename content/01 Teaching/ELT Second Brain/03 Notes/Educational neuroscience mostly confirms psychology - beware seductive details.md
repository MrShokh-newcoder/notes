> Revisiting the Bowers (2016) debate: neuroscience adds little beyond psychology for classroom instruction. Of Lethaby & Harries's three roles (busting neuromyths, informing teaching, validating practice), myth-busting was done by psychology and the rest mostly confirms what we knew; its authority makes us accept claims that flatter our beliefs.

## Sources (key)
Bowers (2016a, b) *Psychological Review* 123(5); Gabrieli (2016); Howard-Jones et al. (2016); Lethaby & Harries (2018); Lethaby, Mayne & Harries (2021) *An Introduction to Evidence-Based Teaching in the English Language Classroom*; Weisberg et al. (2008) "seductive allure of neuroscience explanations".

## Use
- When something is "brain-based", ask for the specific claim and whether the evidence is psychological or classroom data.
- Neuromyth filter: learning styles, left/right brain, Brain Gym, NLP.
- For products: no brain imagery or "brain-friendly" branding as evidence.
