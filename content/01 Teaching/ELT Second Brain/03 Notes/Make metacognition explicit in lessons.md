> Learners often do activities without knowing why, so they stay dependent on teacher and materials ('Materials are usually the authority'). Small routines that surface purpose and strategy build autonomy.

## Moves
- End of lesson: "Which three things were most useful, and why?" Pairs, then groups.
- Ask learners whether an activity was useful and how it could be made more useful.
- Occasionally skip a usual stage, then discuss how that changed their approach.
- Listening text reconstruction: learners circle the words they missed and name the cause. If many struggle, teach connected speech.
- A reflection phase at the end of each coursebook unit.
- Groups make an activity from a reading text for another group.
- Introduce these gradually and don't overdo them.

## Theory
Vandergrift & Goh (2012) *Teaching and Learning Second Language Listening* (metacognitive framework). Nunan (1997) levels of implementation of learner autonomy (check the level numbers against the source). Gilmore (2007) on authenticity. Scrivener's Demand High.

## Limits
A rationale plus ideas from an MA assignment. The only evidence is one informal IELTS class.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- With 7–12-year-olds, do the "most useful thing today" question in L1 with a smiley scale. With IELTS groups, the "name the cause" listening step is a quick diagnostic that pays off immediately.
