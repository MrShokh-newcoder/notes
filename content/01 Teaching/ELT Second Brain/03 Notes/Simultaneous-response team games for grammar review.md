> Two games where every team answers every item. In 'El Picu', four teams write a verb-tense answer within 30 seconds and hold it up together; in the name acrostic duel, teams turn a classmate's name into past-tense verbs or adjectives against the clock.

## El Picu (B2–C1)
- A Gemini-built board (or paper): teams climb a mountain with each correct answer.
- 4 teams, paper and pen; 30 seconds per sentence; all papers up on the signal (no copying).
- Several teams can score per round; a skip button for items that are too hard.

## Name acrostic duel ([post](https://www.cristinacabal.com/?p=16689), B1–C1)
- Word cloud of each team's names; a spinning wheel sets the category (past-tense verb, adjective…).
- LUCAS → *Learned, Understood, Called, Asked, Saw*. First to buzz with a correct acrostic crosses a name off the other team's cloud.
- Doubles as a name-learning game at the start of a course.

## Design note *(Claude)*
The key feature is **simultaneous response**: everyone retrieves every item, unlike hands-up quizzes where one student answers. That's what makes a game retrieval practice rather than entertainment (contrast [[Gamification as pointsification]]).
