> Students confuse processing fluency (it looks familiar) with mastery, and stop working. Four quick tasks (retrieve, explain, mix, transfer) let them see the gap themselves.

## Tasks
1. **Retrieve**: 60 seconds, notes closed, write what you remember.
2. **Explain**: "How do you know?" / "Why does that work?" (self-explanation; Chi et al. 1994 circulatory-system study).
3. **Contrast**: the correct answer beside a near-miss; how do you tell them apart?
4. **Transfer**: a new text, context or variable after practice.
- Frame it as "evidence of what you don't know yet", not as a gotcha.

## Research cited
Processing fluency (Alter & Oppenheimer 2009); retrieval vs restudy (Karpicke & Roediger 2008, *Science*); interleaved maths (Rohrer & Taylor 2007); retrieval and transfer (Butler 2010) *(Claude: names inferred from the linked DOIs; verify)*.

## Limits
- US K-12 framing; the citations are as printed in the article and weren't checked against the primary papers.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- The **contrast** task is ideal for collocation and grammar near-misses (*make/do a mistake*; *since/for*). It's a 2-minute warmer with strong diagnostic value, and it's what Conti calls checking acquisition rather than performance.
