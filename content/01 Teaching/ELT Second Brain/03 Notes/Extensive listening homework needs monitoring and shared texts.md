> A seven-week trial of nightly extensive listening (EL) homework with two pre-intermediate classes. It worked while the teacher checked it; once checks eased, students recycled old entries. Students wanted more guidance and fewer, shared videos.

## Set-up
- Each night: one video or audio (subtitles allowed) + an **EL table**: my response (opinion, difficulties, questions, connections), vocabulary (word family, translation, pronunciation, collocation, own sentence), summary.
- Lessons open with 5 minutes of group sharing; a visible 1/0 completion record.
- Three research-based tips: five videos on one topic in a week (narrow listening); re-watch and note the difference; subtitles first, then without.

## Results (self-report, no control group)
- 257 and 233 entries (~15 per student over 7 weeks).
- Of 25 students: 68% enjoyed EL, 76% felt listening improved, 48% remembered new vocabulary, 56% planned to continue.

## Research cited
Renandya & Farrell (2011); Chang & Millett (2014); Siegel (2011); Stephens (2011); Galloway & Rose (2014).

## Takeaway *(Claude)*
Accountability and choice pull in opposite directions. The fix the data suggest: a **shared weekly text** everyone discusses plus free choice for the rest, so sharing time has something in common to talk about.
