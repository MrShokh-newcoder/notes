> Tasks belong in the zone between what a child can do alone and with help. Scaffolding is temporary, future-oriented support, withdrawn as competence grows. Mis-pitched tasks also cause behaviour problems.

## Claim
Below the ZPD: no new learning, so boredom and disruption. Above it: the gap is too big, so anxiety and disruption. Scaffolding moves the child through the zone and is *handed over* (Bruner) as soon as the child can take it on.

## Theory
Vygotsky (1978, *Mind in Society*): ZPD. Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976): scaffolding. Bruner's "handover principle". Read (2006, 2008) on scaffolding.

## Classroom moves
- Planning test: "achievable with *some* help, but still stretching?"
- Swimming metaphor for fading: armbands → float → unaided.
- One class = many ZPDs, so differentiate the support, not only the task.

## Limits
Conceptual, no evidence; doesn't say how to *diagnose* a ZPD in a class of 25. *(Claude: dynamic assessment, Lantolf & Poehner, is the research answer.)*

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Mis-pitching as a cause of misbehaviour reframes your "weak students are not the problem" video: disruption is diagnostic data about pitch.
- Gamification lead angle: reward systems can *add* support (hints cost coins) and make fading visible to the child.
