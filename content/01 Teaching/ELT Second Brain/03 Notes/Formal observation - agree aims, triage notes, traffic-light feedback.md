> Formal observation is a different skill from peer observation because it has paperwork and stakes. Her first attempts: agree aims and concerns beforehand, condense six pages of notes into one A4 per teacher, rank points with traffic lights, then talk.

## Procedure she arrived at
1. Before: agree lesson aims and the observee's own concerns (she learned to ask this).
2. During: full notes.
3. After: condense to one A4, grouped by category with the DoS; rank with a **traffic-light** system.
4. Feedback meeting; paperwork on time.

## Her honesty
The feedback meeting was the most stressful part; the conversations helped her more than her observees.

## Apply *(Claude)*
- Traffic-lighting is a good antidote to feedback overload: one red, two ambers, the rest green. That matches what we know from written CF, where focused feedback beats comprehensive.
- Contrast with developmental observation (describe before interpreting); the two shouldn't be done by the same person in the same visit.
