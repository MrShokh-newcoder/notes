> Demand High asks whether learners could do more, whether tasks have become rituals, and how to stop 'covering material'. The aim isn't harder tasks ('undoable demand') but a demand 'that comes precisely at the point where the learner is capable of making their next steps forward'.

## Claim
Shift the goal from a *successful task* to *optimal learning*. Keep the same activity, but add a small "tweak" that moves it sideways into the challenge zone. Ask, at any moment: *what is the minimum adjustment that would push learning?*

## Theory
No theorists on the page. Commenters link it to Vygotsky's ZPD and Krashen's i+1. Demand High is a "meme, not a method": "a small course correction to our current direction of travel" ([post](https://demandhighelt.wordpress.com/2013/01/24/demand-high-isnt-a-method-its-a-meme/)). Its lineage is reflective practice and Dewey, not SLA ([ELTChat summary](https://demandhighelt.wordpress.com/2013/01/28/demand-high-vs-dogme-an-eltchat-summary-by-carolyn-kerr/)).

## Demand High vs Dogme
They overlap in exploiting learning opportunities and rejecting routine. Dogme is a materials-light method; Demand High is method- and subject-agnostic and works *with* coursebooks.

## Limits
Critics say it's defined mostly by what it isn't. A 2015 commenter asks how it works with 40 students. Scrivener (2014, *EJAL-TEFL* 3(2)) is the citable article.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- It's the ZPD idea from [[Pitch tasks inside the ZPD and hand over support]] applied at the level of a single exchange rather than the task. A good DELTA M2 PDA focus: "I accept first answers too quickly."
