> Language learning is long and full of setbacks, so learners need to manage their own thoughts, emotions and behaviour. Start with achievable-but-challenging goals, recognising emotional triggers, and positive self-talk; the teacher's long-term job is to hand control over.

## Classroom moves
- Short goal-setting routines (SMART).
- Learners name what triggers anxiety in class and agree one coping strategy.
- Coach positive self-talk explicitly.
- Gradually give choice of tasks and pace; discuss study habits that work.

## Limits
Unreferenced essay; points to an OUP position paper.

## Theory *(Claude)*
Zimmerman's cyclical model: forethought (goals, strategy choice) → performance (self-monitoring) → self-reflection (attribution). Kross's *Chatter* (distanced self-talk, using your own name) is a well-evidenced self-talk technique worth teaching teens before exams.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Teen KET/PET groups: a weekly 3-minute goal card (one skill, one measurable target, one strategy), reviewed the next week.
