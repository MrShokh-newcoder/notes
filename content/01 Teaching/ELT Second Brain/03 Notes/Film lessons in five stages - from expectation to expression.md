> Film is a multimodal text (linguistic, visual, aural, gestural, spatial), so lessons should teach viewing and representing as well as the four skills. Film English structures every lesson in five stages.

## The five stages
1. **Expectation** (pre-viewing): theme discussion, a quotation, prediction from the title, a glossary.
2. **Exploration** (while-viewing): several viewings, each with a new purpose; literal and inferential questions answered with evidence from speech, gesture, tone, sound.
3. **Evaluation:** how is meaning built? Discussion, reflection, role-play.
4. **Enhancement:** a related article revisits the vocabulary in writing.
5. **Expression:** learners make their own multimodal text (vlog, infographic, comic), usually homework.
- Typical lesson: B1–B2, "mature teens and adults", 90–120 min (e.g., [Growing Up](https://film-english.com/esl-lesson-plan-growing-up/)).

## Theory named
Vygotsky; Mayer's multimedia learning; Sweller's cognitive load; Krashen's affective filter; Kress; Lim & Tan-Chia (2022).

## Read critically *(Claude)*
A commercial framework; "research shows" claims are uncited. Note the tension: cognitive load theory warns that redundant channels (fast speech + dense subtitles + images) can *raise* load for lower levels. Short clips, one purpose per viewing.
