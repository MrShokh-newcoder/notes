> Summarising Katrien Deroey's corpus work: EAP coursebooks teach neat 'useful phrase' lists, but real lecturers flag importance with markers like 'Remember…', 'The point is…', 'The key thing is…', often in reduced connected speech. Coursebooks under-teach them.

## Classroom implications
- Check materials against real lecture transcripts (BASE, MICASE) or local lecturers' recordings.
- Teach importance markers explicitly, *including* how they sound in connected speech (linking, elision, intonation).
- Have learners use the same markers in their own presentations.

## Sources (as given)
Deroey, K. (2021) NFEAP talk; Gilmore (2015) "Research into practice…", *Language Teaching* 48(4). Further reading *(Claude)*: Deroey & Taverniers (2012) on lexicogrammatical relevance markers in lectures, *English for Specific Purposes*.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- IELTS Listening Part 4 is a lecture; answers often follow exactly these markers. Train "marker → answer coming" as a listening strategy.
