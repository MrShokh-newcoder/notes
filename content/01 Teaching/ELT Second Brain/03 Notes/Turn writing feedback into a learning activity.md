> Detailed written feedback is often ignored, AI feedback included. 'Feedback is of little or no value unless learners learn from it.' Build a composite essay from the group's recurring errors, have teams score it against criteria, add an AI audio overview, then revise their own essays.

## Procedure
1. Build a **composite essay** from the group's recurring problems, altered enough that no one's text is recognisable.
2. Generate an audio "podcast" overview (Google NotebookLM) with the success criteria (Content, Structure, Accuracy) in the prompt.
3. Teams score the essay 1–3 per criterion with descriptors, cite evidence, and write "moving forward" points.
4. Play the podcast; teams add new points.
5. Discuss; for homework, revise your own essay using the feedforward points.

## Theory
Kerr (2020) *Giving Feedback to Language Learners* (Cambridge Papers in ELT): "Learning from feedback cannot be forced: the teacher's task is to try to create the right conditions."

## Limits
Setup takes ~1 hour; fast podcast hosts suit intermediate+; privacy when uploading essays isn't addressed.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- The composite essay alone (no AI needed) is a strong IELTS Task 2 routine. It makes feedback a task, matching Butler's "comments over grades" and Kerr's conditions-for-uptake point.
