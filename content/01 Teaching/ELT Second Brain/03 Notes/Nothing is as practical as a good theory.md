> Recipes and tricks are of limited use without a theory of language and learning. Dellar's came from corpora: language is often fixed, collocation is central, and grammar and vocabulary intertwine. 'Innovation is simply change for the sake of change' without it.

## The teacher's job, from his theory
1. Make sure students meet language that's useful to them (and know why).
2. Make it intelligible: explain and exemplify.
3. Help them notice salient features.
4. Get them using it.
5. Revise as much as possible later.
6. Use routine to notice interlanguage problems at the margins.

## Theory
Corpus findings on fixedness and collocation; lexical priming (Hoey): each encounter primes expectations. Related: Scrivener & Underhill's Demand High.

## Limits
A theory makes practice "more static". He accepts that cost.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- This is the strongest argument for the second-brain project itself: building a coherent theory of learning that your techniques, reels and checklists are derived from.
- For the teacher checklist at school: one item could be "can state the learning principle behind each stage". It tests theory, not tricks.
