**Highlights / notes file:** [[The Anxious Generation - Jonathan Haidt]]

> Childhood shifted from play-based to phone-based around 2010–2015 (front cameras, Instagram, likes). Smartphones are 'experience blockers' that crowd out free play, attunement and social learning while the teen brain's self-control systems are still maturing.

## From my notes
- Reward-seeking brain areas mature early; the frontal cortex (self-control, delay of gratification) isn't at full capacity until the mid-20s.
- Timeline: WWW 1989 → Facebook 2004 → iPhone 2007 → like/retweet 2009 → front camera 2010 → Instagram bought 2012 → Snapchat stories 2013 → TikTok 2016–17.
- Five features of childhood: slow growth, free play, attunement, social learning (imitation), expectant brains and sensitive periods.
- "We are overprotecting children in the real world while underprotecting them online."
- Discover mode vs defend mode; kids are antifragile.

## The debate *(Claude)*
Haidt's causal claim is contested: Odgers (2024, *Nature* review) and Orben & Przybylski argue the correlations between social media use and adolescent mental health are small and causation unproven. Treat the classroom implications (phone-free lessons, more face-to-face interaction) as low-risk regardless of who's right.

## Apply in my context *(Claude)*
- Phone-free lessons for KET/PET teens; collaborative, embodied tasks; school policy conversations with parents framed around attention, not panic.
