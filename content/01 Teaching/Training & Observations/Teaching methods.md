Teaching methods refer to the ways in which a teacher delivers instruction and engages students in the learning process. Different teaching methods can be used to meet the diverse learning needs of students, and to create an environment that is conducive to learning. Some of the most common teaching methods include:

• **Lecture method**: The teacher presents information to the class in a formal and structured way. This method is often used to present new information or to review material that has already been covered.

• **Discussion method**: The teacher leads a group discussion in which students share ideas, ask questions, and engage in dialogue about a particular topic. This method is often used to encourage critical thinking and to develop social skills.

• **Inquiry-based learning**: This method of teaching encourages students to explore, question and investigate on their own. The teacher acts as a guide and facilitator, helping students to develop critical thinking skills and to make connections between new information and what they already know.

• **Project-based learning**: This method involves students working on a long-term project or problem, often in small groups. The project is designed to be open-ended and encourages students to explore multiple perspectives and develop critical thinking skills.

• **Hands-on learning**: This method involves students actively participating in activities that are designed to be hands-on and experiential. This method can be used to teach a wide range of subjects, from science to math to history.

• **Game-based learning**: This method uses games and other interactive activities to engage students and make learning more fun. Games can be used to teach a wide range of subjects, from math and science to social studies and language arts.

• **Flipped Classroom**: This method involves students watching video lectures or reading assigned materials before coming to class. Class time is then used for discussion, interactive activities, and problem-solving.

• **Online learning**: This method involves students learning online through a variety of platforms such as websites, apps, and learning management systems. This method allows for a great deal of flexibility and can be used for both synchronous and asynchronous learning.

These are just a few examples of the many different teaching methods that are available to educators. The best teaching method is often one that is tailored to the specific needs and learning styles of the students in the class

**Project-based learning (PBL)** is a teaching method in which students work on a long-term project or problem, often in small groups. This method is designed to be open-ended and encourages students to explore multiple perspectives and develop critical thinking skills. Here are some examples of project-based learning activities that could be used with beginner level English language learners:

• Create a travel brochure: Students work in small groups to research and create a brochure about a city or country where English is spoken. They should include information about the culture, history, and tourist attractions. They can also create a short skit or role-play as a tour guide to present their brochure to the class.

• Host a cultural fair: Students research a country or culture where English is spoken, and create a display or presentation to share with the class. They can also prepare traditional foods and dress up in traditional clothing to present during the fair.

• Create a podcast: Students work in small groups to research and create a podcast on a topic of interest. They should use English throughout the podcast and can include music and sound effects to make it more engaging.

• Make a movie: Students work in groups to write, direct, and act in a short film in English. They can choose a topic of their interest and can use props and costumes to make the movie more interesting.

• Design a restaurant: Students work in groups to design a restaurant and create a menu in English. They should also create a skit or role-play where they act out a customer ordering food and a server taking the order.

• Create a news show: Students work in groups to research and report on current events in English. They can create a news show with anchors, reporters, and weather forecast.

These are just a few examples of project-based learning activities that could be used with beginner level English language learners. The key is to make sure that the project is tailored to the students' level of language proficiency and is designed to be challenging yet achievable. Maintaining student motivation can be challenging, especially for beginner level English language learners. However, there are several practical strategies that teachers can use to keep students motivated and engaged in the learning process:

• Set clear and achievable goals: Helping students set clear and achievable goals for their learning can help to increase their motivation. Be sure to provide regular feedback on their progress and celebrate their successes along the way.

• Create a positive learning environment: A positive learning environment can help to increase student motivation by making them feel safe, respected, and valued. Creating a classroom culture that values effort, promotes cooperation, and encourages risk-taking.

• Use varied teaching methods: Using a variety of teaching methods can help to keep students engaged and motivated. Incorporating activities that are interactive, hands-on, and visually stimulating can help to capture students' attention and interest.

• Provide choice and autonomy: Giving students choices and autonomy over their learning can help to increase their motivation. For example, allowing students to choose their own topics for research projects or to work on a project in small groups can help to increase their engagement and motivation.

• Provide immediate feedback: Providing students with immediate feedback on their work can help to increase their motivation. Feedback should be specific, positive, and actionable, and should help students to see their progress and understand what they need to do to improve.

• Offer incentives and rewards: Offering incentives and rewards for good work or behavior can help to increase student motivation. These incentives could include things like extra credit, special privileges, or small rewards such as stickers.

• Encourage self-reflection: Encouraging students to reflect on their learning can help to increase their motivation. This can be done through self-reflection activities such as journaling, metacognitive strategies, and goal-setting.

• Make learning fun: Incorporating fun and engaging activities can help to keep students motivated. Games, songs, and other interactive activities can be used to make learning more enjoyable.

• Personalize the learning: Creating personalized learning experiences can help to increase student motivation. This can be done by tailoring instruction to meet the individual needs and interests of each student.

• Use technology: Utilizing technology such as interactive whiteboards, tablets, and educational apps can help to capture students' attention and engage them in the learning process.

It's worth to mention that motivation can be affected by many factors such as students' background, culture, and prior knowledge. Therefore, it's important to be aware of and sensitive to these factors when implementing these strategies.
