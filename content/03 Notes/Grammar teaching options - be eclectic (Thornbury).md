> A summary of a Thornbury talk: you're teaching grammar even when you think you aren't (recasts are implicit grammar teaching). Inductive/deductive and explicit/implicit are options, not camps. 'Be eclectic. It's not an either-or decision.'

## Decision points
- Planned (syllabus) vs reactive (error-driven) grammar.
- Implicit feedback (recasts, reformulation) to protect communication vs explicit (draw attention) when the gap needs noticing.
- Inductive lesson: a model text, picture or storyboard; ask learners for *reasons* ("Why this form here?"), not only rules.
- Test rules against learners' lives. (A student objected that he never makes "spontaneous decisions", so the *will*-for-spontaneous-decisions rule didn't fit.)

## Sources
Thornbury (1997) "Reformulation and reconstruction: tasks that promote 'noticing'", *ELT Journal* 51(4), 326–335.
