> Assertive Discipline and 'cooperative' programmes alike (rewards, logical consequences) aim at compliance. 'When a student doesn't do what he is told, the problem may be with what he has been told to do.'

## Claim
Rewards and consequences produce compliance without ethical understanding. The deeper fix is curriculum engagement and involving students in making the principles. Teachers should examine their own need for control.

## Targets
Canter, *Assertive Discipline*; Dreikurs & Grey, *Logical Consequences*; Albert, *Cooperative Discipline*; Curwin & Mendler, *Discipline with Dignity*.

## Limits *(Claude)*
- No outcome data for his alternative.
- Doesn't engage the PBIS / school-wide positive behaviour support evidence, which shows reductions in office referrals.
- "Give up control" is hard to implement where a school must meet a behaviour standard.
