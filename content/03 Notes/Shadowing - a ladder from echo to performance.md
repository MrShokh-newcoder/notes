> Shadowing (repeating a recording a fraction of a second behind the speaker) trains real-time speech processing, prosody and fluency at once, needs almost no preparation, and works with any short authentic audio.

## Procedure
1. 30–60 seconds of audio; listen for gist.
2. Transcript; clarify vocabulary; shadow while reading.
3. Repeat focusing on rhythm and stress, not words; remove the transcript.
- **Shadowing ladder:** echo → supported (transcript) → guided (no transcript) → natural (authentic audio) → performance (record, compare, repeat over days).
- **Week routine:** Mon listen for meaning · Tue shadow with transcript · Wed without · Thu record and compare · Fri repeat.
- Avoid: fast recordings, too much unknown vocabulary, correcting mid-shadow.

## Sources listed
Kadota (2019) *Shadowing as a practice in SLA*; Tamai (2005); Hamada (2018); Field (2008); Gilbert (2008); Underhill (2005); Nation & Newton (2009).

## Research note *(Claude)*
Most shadowing studies are Japanese EFL university samples (Hamada; Kadota), showing gains in bottom-up decoding and listening scores more clearly than in speaking. Treat it primarily as a listening/phonological-loop exercise with a pronunciation bonus.
