> In speaking, respond to the message first and give form feedback later. Tsateri admits this rests on intuition: the research she quotes (Kerr 2020) says there's 'no clear consensus' on immediate vs delayed correction.

## Practice
Let the learner finish; respond to the content; ask genuine follow-ups; note errors; do form feedback afterwards or in an "upgrade" stage.

## Sources
Kerr (2020) *Giving Feedback to Language Learners* (Cambridge Papers in ELT), citing Doughty (2001), Ellis & Shintani (2013); Korthagen (2017) *Teachers and Teaching* 23(4) on emotion in learning.

## Limits
Moves from "no consensus" to a firm stance on intuition. Doesn't address learners who want immediate correction, exam classes, or fossilisation risk.

## Where this sits *(Claude)*
- Tsateri (connect first) ↔ Underhill (upgrade *during* the flow, [[Upgrade language during fluency work with minimal interference]]) ↔ Fluency First (both can work, [[Reactive focus on form - choose few, global, developmental errors]]). The evidence supports Fluency First: timing matters less than *selectivity* and *noticeability*.
