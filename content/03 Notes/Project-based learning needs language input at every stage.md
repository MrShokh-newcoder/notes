> PBL adapts to ELT as a staged framework, but learners can't be expected to already have the language a project needs. 'Providing language input is a key to a successful PBL environment.'

## Stages
Generate & stimulate → define & refine (a learner-worded driving question that "can't be Googled") → designate & collaborate (SMART goals, visible roles) → compare & share (THINK mnemonic for peer feedback) → enhance & advance → review & revise → produce & present (to an audience beyond the class).
- A rotating **English Captain** per group keeps the group working in English.

## Theory
Buck Institute PBL definition; Lightbown (2014) *Focus on Content-Based Language Teaching* ("much language is acquired through meaningful use"); MMM (Meet, Manipulate, Make it your own), cited via Brewster, Ellis & Girard, *The Primary English Teacher's Guide*.

## Limits
Written for teens and adults, with YL use adapted; nothing on low-level vocabulary or assessment.
