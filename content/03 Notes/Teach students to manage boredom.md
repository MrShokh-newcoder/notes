> Teachers own the job of making lessons engaging, but students can also learn to manage boredom: notice it, then attend to a deeper feature of the task. A one-lesson growth-mindset routine.

## Lesson
1. Review fixed vs growth mindset.
2. Read aloud a short text on boredom ('satiation').
3. Agree norms: thinking or privately saying "this is boring" is fine; shouting it isn't.
4. Students list their challenges and how a growth mindset would help with each.
5. Practise: notice the feeling, then look for a deeper feature of the task.

## Limits
The research on satiation is unsourced; no evidence the lesson worked. Growth-mindset interventions show small average effects (Sisk et al. 2018) *(Claude)*.

## Better framing *(Claude)*
Boredom research (Pekrun's control-value theory) says boredom rises when tasks are too easy *or* too hard and when value is low. Fix challenge level and relevance first; the self-regulation lesson is the second line.
