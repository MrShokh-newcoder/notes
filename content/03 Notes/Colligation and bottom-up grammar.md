> Many 'grammar' errors (*I am agree*, *it is depend of*, *I feel myself sad*) are micro-grammar: ignorance of a word's grammatical company (colligation), not failure to apply big rules. Teach the pattern with the word.

## Claim
Summary of Hugh Dellar's IATEFL webinar: "the crux of the Lexical Approach is a different approach to teaching grammar". Lewis (2000: 149–150) called it more grammatical than a structural syllabus.

## Theory
Firth (collocation, colligation); Hoey's lexical priming (L1 primings explain transfer); Lewis (ed.) 2000 *Teaching Collocation*; Willis, *Rules, Patterns and Words*.

## Procedure
1. Teach words *with* their patterns: *it depends (on)*, *I agree (with)*, not *depend* + definition.
2. Reformulate learner output with natural examples.
3. In correction, target word grammar, not tense.
4. Use learner-dictionary pattern data (*there is (no) possibility that…*, *the possibility of…*).
5. Contrast with L1 where useful.

## Limits & critique
Geoff Jordan (comments): what counts as a "pattern", how many are there, and why is bottom-up better? Selivan: "hundreds or thousands… don't quote me on that." The argument is largely assertion.
