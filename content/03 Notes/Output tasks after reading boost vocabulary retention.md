> After reading, any written output task using the new words beat reading alone, at both immediate and delayed post-test. Prediction tasks beat question-answering, which beat summarising.

## Study
Rassaei, E. (2017). Effects of three forms of reading-based output activity on L2 vocabulary learning. *Language Teaching Research*, 21(1), 76–95.
- Learners read a story containing pre-tested unknown words, glossed in L1 (Farsi).
- Four groups: summary / question-and-answer / prediction / reading only.
- Post-test and delayed post-test. Sample size not reported in the summary.

## Findings
All three output groups > control at both tests. **Prediction** was significantly best at both points; Q&A came second. Explanations: Output Hypothesis (Swain), **depth of processing** (Craik & Lockhart; Laufer & Hulstijn's Involvement Load is the closer construct *(Claude)*), retrieval frequency.

## Classroom moves
- Follow every reading text with a short written task that *requires* the target words.
- Prefer tasks that make learners **apply** the words: predict what happens next, or transfer them to a new situation.
- Check with a delayed test, not only an immediate one.
- Gloss before the task.

## Limits
Narrative texts only; a single study; context only lightly described. Non-fiction or evaluative tasks may behave differently.
