> Pre-schoolers need calm, positive handling, and as much time on social and motor skills as on English. Young learners need predictable routines, small choices and visibly fair treatment.

## Very young learners (Elliott)
- Alternate engaging and calming activities; attention spans are ~5 minutes at most.
- Repeat favourite songs, stories and routines; add harder material when they tire of them.
- Small, single-step tasks; praise the behaviour you want.
- You don't need a puppet; your face, voice and flashcards do the same job.

## Young learners, ~7–12 ([Gowers](https://ihteachenglish.com/resources/six-top-tips-for-new-younger-learner-teachers/))
- Fixed routines at the start, during instructions, and at the end.
- Small choices (where to sit, which of two activities).
- Repeat target language with variation (new partner, whisper, shout).
- Apply rewards and attention **evenly**: perceived unfairness is children's commonest complaint.
- Accept some noise (e.g. a running dictation), but have a clear end signal; move on early if the class is restless.
