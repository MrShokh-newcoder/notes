> Eliciting keeps the thinking with learners and engages retrieval. It fails when there's no context, too many possible answers, or the teacher fills the silence. Count to eight; then accept, shape, or tell.

## Six steps
1. Could a student realistically produce this? If not, present it.
2. Build context first (situation, picture, mini-story) to narrow answers to one or two.
3. A context students care about.
4. A question with only one answer (CCQ-style), in graded language.
5. Wait ~8 seconds, make eye contact, then nominate.
6. Accept / praise and shape / tell after ~2 attempts. Then drill and board it.
- "A clean model beats a long, awkward extraction, and there's no shame in it."

## Theory *(Claude)*
Wait time (Rowe) and the generation effect (producing an answer aids memory) support it; the 8-second figure is a heuristic.
