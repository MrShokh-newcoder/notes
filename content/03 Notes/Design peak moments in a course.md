> Courses are remembered by their peaks and endings, not their length. Plan a strong first day, celebrated milestones, a real 'game' (performance to an audience) and a memorable ending, and treat bad moments as chances for recovery.

## Course design moves *(Claude)*
- **First lesson as a peak:** a personal welcome, a surprising activity, early visible success.
- **Milestones:** 100 words learned, first full mock, 10th graded reader: name and mark them.
- **The game:** an end-of-unit performance with an audience (parents' showcase, a debate, a speaking club demo, a class magazine).
- **Endings:** the last 5 minutes of a lesson and the last week of a course get disproportionate weight; plan them.
- **Pits:** a failed mock is a defining moment either way; a structured recovery conversation can make it positive.

## Tension
Raising stakes and public performance can raise anxiety for some learners; offer roles (presenter, editor, host).
