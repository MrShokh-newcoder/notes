> Judge any task by Nation's eight principles: Motivation, Self-efficacy (engagement); Focus, Accuracy (usefulness); Repetition, Time-on-task (quantity); Elaboration, Analysis (quality). Good techniques satisfy several at once.

## The principles
| Group | Principles |
|---|---|
| Motivation & engagement | Motivation · Self-efficacy |
| Focus & usefulness | Focus (attend to what is to be learned) · Accuracy |
| Quantity | Repetition · Time on task |
| Quality | Elaboration (rich, varied encounters) · Analysis (word parts, patterns) |

Sources: Nation (2024) *The 20 Most Effective Language Teaching Techniques*, ch. 4; Nation (2024) "Re-thinking the principles of (vocabulary) learning and their applications", *Languages* 9(5), 160 ([open access](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-471X/9/5/160)). Smith's [summary of the paper](https://frenchteachernet.blogspot.com/2026/07/summary-of-2024-paper-by-paul-nation-on.html) was ChatGPT-assisted and then edited, so check against the paper.

## Smith's comments
- He'd like more on working memory and cognitive load.
- Analysis matters less for A1–A2 novices; he'd "downplay the analysis".
- Incidental learning through extensive reading and listening is vital. He doubts learning words for their own sake.
- Nation cites "at least 6 or 7 encounters" for a word, and 95–98% known words for comprehension.
