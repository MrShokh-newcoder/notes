> Jordan argues that Li Wei's (2018) translanguaging fails as a theory: vague criteria, no operationalised constructs, a relativist epistemology at odds with the scientific standards it claims, and unoriginal 'Translanguaging Space' and 'Instinct' ideas.

## Criteria for a good theory (Jordan)
Explanatory power, testability, clarity, coherence, consistency, economy, breadth, usefulness. Constructs must be defined, and the problem the theory solves must be stated.

## Sources
Li Wei (2018) *Applied Linguistics* 39(1); Fodor (1975, 1983); Popper (1972); Gregg (1993, 1996); N. Ellis (2002); Jordan (2004) *Theory Construction in SLA*; Phillipson's monolingual, native-speaker and subtractive fallacies.

## Counter-arguments (comments)
Kevin Gregg corrected Jordan's account of Fodor (accepted). Others argue named languages can't be dropped (Mura) and that teachers' power to "transform structures" is overstated (Nick).

## Key distinction *(Claude)*
Critiquing translanguaging as a *theory of language* doesn't touch translanguaging as a *pedagogy*. Planned L1 use can work (see [[Planned translanguaging improved expository reading (MultiEd)]]) whatever its theoretical status. Keep the two levels apart in DELTA essays.
