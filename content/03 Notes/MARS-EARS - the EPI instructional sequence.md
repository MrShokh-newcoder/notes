> Extensive Processing Instruction runs Modelling, Awareness-raising, Receptive processing, Structured production, Expansion, Autonomous recall, then Routinisation and Spontaneity. Input-heavy, chunk-based and recycled across a whole term.

## Claim
A unit lasts a term with 5 sub-units. Sub-units 1–4 each run a MARSEA cycle on new material. Sub-unit 5 is Routinisation + Spontaneity, interweaving the unit with earlier units. Recycling core items across contexts is non-negotiable.

## The sequence
1. **M**odelling: chunks and patterns presented in sentence builders.
2. **A**wareness-raising: "pop-up grammar" / "pop-up phonics", brief pointers to features.
3. **R**eceptive processing: many listening/reading tasks on the same patterns. Narrow listening/reading at ≥90% comprehensible input. Listening *for learning* before listening for testing (Listening-As-Modelling).
4. **S**tructured production: chunking-aloud games (Sentence Stealer, Mind-reading, Lie Detector), then structured retrieval, then student-run oral games.
5. **E**xpansion: explicit morphosyntax *after* chunks are processed (deductive, guided discovery or inductive).
6. **A**utonomous recall: short, low-stakes checks (3 receptive, 2 productive).
7. **R**outinisation & **S**pontaneity: timed fluency with familiar language, then unplanned tasks under time pressure ("Real Operating Conditions").

## Mechanism / theory
Implicit–explicit synergy, input flooding and enhancement, depth of processing (Leow 2015); Nation's fluency strand; Transfer Appropriate Processing; interference and decay; Bjork's "new theory of disuse".

## Limits & critique
- Long: structured production alone may need 1.5–2 lessons with average groups. Fluency can't come from "a couple of lessons".
- A description of practice, not an outcome study. Partisan framing ("the real Conti method").
- Assumes 20–24 lessons a term and whole-curriculum recycling.
- **Transfer caveat:** written for UK secondary MFL (French/Spanish/German) with ~2–3 lessons a week and minimal out-of-class exposure. Tashkent EFL learners get far more ambient English (YouTube, games), so input-scarcity arguments apply less strongly. *(Claude)*
