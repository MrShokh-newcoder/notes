> Listening is hard because input is transient, working memory is small, connected speech blurs words, form and meaning compete for attention, and coverage below ~95–98% collapses comprehension. Anxiety and low self-efficacy compound all of this.

## Cognitive barriers
Transient input; limited working memory; assimilation and elision; competing demands of form vs meaning; the comprehensibility threshold (Nation & Newton 2009: 95–98%); noticing (Schmidt 1990).

## Affective barriers
Listening anxiety; self-efficacy (Bandura 1997; Graham 2007, *Language Learning Journal* 35(1)); missing visual and social cues (gesture helps: Huang, Kim & Christianson 2019).

## Implications
Model how listening works rather than testing it; design tasks that force careful, intensive listening; teach word recognition and chunks; the teacher as live input source (control speed, emphasis, gesture); build self-efficacy through scaffolded, low-stakes listening.

## Limits
Several claims are uncited ("sounds disappear within two seconds", "3–5 items" in working memory).
