> Nation proposes roughly equal time for four strands: meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning and fluency development. His 2024 book assesses 20 techniques against them.

## The strands
1. **Meaning-focused input**: reading and listening at ~95–98% known words.
2. **Meaning-focused output**: speaking and writing to communicate.
3. **Language-focused learning**: deliberate attention to form (pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar).
4. **Fluency development**: easy, familiar material under pressure to go faster (e.g. **4/3/2**, 10-minute writing).

## Techniques in the book (as named)
4/3/2; 10-minute writing; flashcards; substitution tables; graded readers; dictation (several types, incl. delayed dictation and delayed copying as mental rehearsal); guided writing; intensive reading; problem-solving tasks.

## Smith's verdict
"Not a book to recommend for modern/world language teachers, but useful additional reading for ESL teachers." Most techniques suit A2+. The substitution-table chapter lacks the EPI insights; some argue 25% language focus is too much.
