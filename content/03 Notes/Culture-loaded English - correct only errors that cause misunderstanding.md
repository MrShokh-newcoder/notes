> Learners need not chase native-like perfection and can keep their cultural identity in English ("Czenglish" is a stage, not a crime). Teachers should still flag the 'tricky' L1-driven errors that cause embarrassing or serious misunderstanding.

## Ideas from the P.A.R.K. conference talks
- **TENOR** (Simon Gill): "teaching English for no obvious reason". Ask learners why they are learning; motive shapes what accuracy matters.
- **Czenglish** (Mark Andrews): L1-shaped English as a topic to discuss, not to police item by item.
- Activity: give a list of L1 expressions with no word-for-word English equivalent; learners must explain them in English with context.

## Limits
A conference report; "tricky" errors are never defined, so the line between tolerable and harmful transfer is left to teacher judgement.

## A working criterion *(Claude)*
Use Jenkins's Lingua Franca Core logic for pronunciation and Burt & Kiparsky's **global vs local** errors for grammar and lexis: correct what blocks meaning or causes offence; tolerate what is merely marked.
- Uzbek/Russian examples worth flagging: false friends (*magazine* for shop, *accurate* for neat), *I am agree*, direct-translation politeness that sounds rude.
