> 'Teacher trainers and educators don't tell pre-service or practicing teachers about the research findings on interlanguage development.' Instructed-SLA research favours implicit, meaning-focused learning over the presentation and practice of pre-selected items, so PPP coursebook teaching persists against the evidence.

## Claim
CELTA-type courses and influential trainers (Ur, Harmer, Dellar & Walkley) teach PPP and coursebook-driven ELT without examining whether it works.

## Research cited
Doughty (2003) "Instructed SLA" in Doughty & Long (eds) *Handbook of SLA*; Whong, Gil & Marsden (2014) *SLR* 30(4); Bryfonski & McKay (2017) TBLT meta-analysis, *LTR*; Long (2015) *SLA and TBLT*; Corder (1967), Selinker (1972), Long (1983).

## What he proposes
- Teach SLA findings early (his sketch: ~15% of a course on how people learn an L2), then approaches, the language, management, syllabus design.
- Meaning-first teaching: comprehensible input, participation, feedback. TBLT as the alternative.
- Five questions for trainers, e.g. "What is your view of the English language?" "What types of syllabus do you discuss?"

## Counter-arguments (comments)
- Heyman: teachers know the theory but lack workable ways to implement TBLT; the coursebook is the path of least resistance.
- McCutcheon: pre-service trainees lack the language expertise for good focus on form, so they end up with "a bastardised version" of TBLT.
- Exam washback and institutional constraints aren't addressed *(Claude)*.
