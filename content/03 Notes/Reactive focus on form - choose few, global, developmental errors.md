> In fluency-led and task-based teaching, language is addressed reactively. Target a few consistent, global or developmental errors rather than a 'smorgasbord'. 'Both immediate and delayed correction may be beneficial' (Ellis & Shintani), so the fear that correction always breaks fluency is overstated.

## Selection rules
- **Global** errors (meaning breaks down) before local ones.
- **Consistent** errors (a developmental stage) before slips the learner clearly "knows".
- **Upgrades** for over-used or non-idiomatic forms ("binge-watched" rather than "watched many episodes").

## Delivery
- Implicit: a brief recast close to the error so the learner can "notice the gap".
- Explicit correction may work better for simple rules, and some learners prefer it.
- Capture formats: slips for early finishers; board for delayed feedback; shared doc with comments.
- The authors' **ICE** framework: Identify → Capture → Exploit.
