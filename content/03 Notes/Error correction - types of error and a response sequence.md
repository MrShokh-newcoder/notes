> 'Errors are evidence of learner development.' Decide case by case whether to correct, respond to meaning as well as form, and vary and explain your techniques.

## Error types
Slip vs systematic error vs attempt; overt vs covert; L1 transfer vs intralingual/developmental. By area: pronunciation, lexical, semantic, grammatical, pragmatic, receptive.

## A response sequence
1. Ask for repetition. This invites self-correction and buys thinking time.
2. Self-correction → peer correction.
3. If no one can, give the answer or a prompt. Don't labour an item learners can't produce yet.
4. Everyone repeats, then the original speaker repeats.
5. When you're *not* correcting (fluency phase), tell learners why.
6. Ask of each utterance: *Is this adequate? Can I get more? Do I want more?* (Demand High)
7. Negotiate with learners: correction during or after speaking?

## Theory
Lightbown & Spada (2006: 125–128): explicit correction, recasts, clarification requests, metalinguistic feedback, elicitation, repetition. Their summary of Lyster & Ranta (1997) *(Claude)*. Scrivener, *Learning Teaching* (Toolkit 2); Harmer, *Practice of ELT* ch. 8.
