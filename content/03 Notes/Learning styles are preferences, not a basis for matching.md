> Harmer presents VAK learning styles as preferences and Gardner's multiple intelligences as a reason not to label learners 'unintelligent'. Keep the second point; drop the idea of matching teaching to a student's style, which the research doesn't support.

## What the evidence says *(Claude)*
- **Meshing hypothesis** (teach visual learners visually, etc.) has no good support: Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork (2008); Rogowsky, Calhoun & Tallal (2015).
- Gardner himself says multiple intelligences are **not** learning styles (2013).
- What *does* help: matching the mode to the **content** (pictures for concrete nouns, audio for pronunciation) and using several modes for everyone (dual coding, Paivio; Mayer).

## How to talk about it in DELTA *(Claude)*
Preferences exist and affect motivation; using varied modes is good practice; claims that matching style to instruction improves learning are unsupported. That's the defensible Module 1/Module 3 position.
