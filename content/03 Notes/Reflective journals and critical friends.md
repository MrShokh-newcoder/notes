> Journal writing builds awareness of *what* you do, *how* and *why*. Sharing journals with a few trusted peers, a small community of practice, multiplies the benefit. 'It is not a panacea.'

## Procedure
- Write after each class, or weekly if daily is too much: successes, failures, fears.
- Share with 4–5 trusted peers (one cohort did this over 12 weeks); peers comment and challenge.
- No group? Start with one or two critical friends.
- Use writing to separate emotion from analysis of the lesson.

## Theory
Farrell, *Reflective Language Teaching: From Research to Practice*. "Community of practice" (Lave & Wenger, uncited in the post *(Claude)*).

## Related: small, SMART PD ([2013](https://eltrantsreviewsreflections.wordpress.com/next-steps-in-professional-development-workshop-materials/))
"Small changes are more likely to stick." Critique vague plans ("I'm going to work harder this fall") and rewrite them as SMART. Ask "How are you a different teacher than this time last year?" Reading: Foord, *The Developing Teacher*.

## Limits
Anecdotal; competition and fear of judgement block sharing.
