> In government English-medium primary schools in India, structured, *planned* use of home languages alongside English improved reading comprehension of expository texts in one region, with no perceptible harm elsewhere.

## Study
MultiEd (2023–26), lead Ianthi M. Tsimpli (Cambridge). British Council (2026), [doi:10.57884/6PDN-BN90](https://doi.org/10.57884/6PDN-BN90).
- MultiPed intervention: 6–7 months; 37 teachers, 8 schools, 694 pupils (Delhi and Assam).
- Expository (EVS) reading comprehension improved in the Assam intervention group; narrative results were mixed.
- Teacher-talk corpus: English-matrix sentences in ~60% of utterances.

## Moves
Staged reading lessons with multilingual vocabulary support; pre-set pauses on meaningful chunks; **planned** (not spontaneous) home-language clarification; comprehension checks in both languages.

## Related position paper
[Language-responsive education](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/publications/case-studies-insights-and-research/language-responsive-education) (2026): language-in-education decisions must be local. Strong foundations in a familiar language *and* English; English-medium transitions fail without teacher proficiency, materials and assessment.

## Limits
Small and non-randomised; teacher attitudes are self-reported; the funder evaluated its own programme; India's policy context differs.
