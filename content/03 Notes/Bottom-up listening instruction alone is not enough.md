> 22 hours of bottom-up skills instruction helped some listeners and not others, depending on their prior listening style. Bottom-up work needs to be combined with top-down and metacognitive work.

## Study
Yeldham, M., & Gruba, P. *Toward an instructional approach to developing interactive second language listening.* *Language Teaching Research*, 18(1), 33–53. (The reference list says 2014, the body says 2013. Check before citing; vol. 18 corresponds to 2014.)
- 5 Taiwanese university EFL learners; a 22-hour bottom-up course over ~1.5 semesters.
- Multiple case study: tests, recall, verbal reports, interviews.

## Findings (qualitative)
- The **top-down** listener improved substantially, as bottom-up skills curbed over-guessing. Segmentation still didn't improve.
- One bottom-up listener gained a lot (possibly confounded by outside test prep). Another still struggled.
- The **interactive** listener's total rose, but top-down use *fell*.
- Conclusion: bottom-up instruction is "inadequate" alone; bottom-up and top-down processing need to interact.

## Classroom moves
- Pair segmentation, stress and discourse-marker work with prediction and inferencing.
- Teach the metacognitive cycle: plan/predict → monitor → evaluate → problem-solve (Vandergrift & Goh). Have learners reflect on what misled them.
- Gap-completion: finish sentence endings, guess masked segments (noise over the recording).
- Diagnose each learner's tendency (top-down guesser vs bottom-up decoder).

## Limits
n = 5, one setting, no comparison group, a possible confound.
