> Kerr reviews Moorhouse & Wong's Cambridge Element *Generative AI and Language Teaching* (2025) as promotional: thin on practice, selective on evidence, and built on the unexamined assumption that GenAI makes teaching more efficient. 'AI for Good' framing echoes a UN initiative sponsored by big vendors.

## Sources cited
Bender & Hanna (2025) *The AI Con*; Selwyn, Ljungqvist & Sonesson (2025) on teachers' work around GenAI's frailties; OECD (2023) *Generative AI in the Classroom: From Hype to Reality*; Adams (2025).

## Implications
- Ask for evidence behind "AI saves teacher time" (e.g., grading) and test it in your context.
- Check who sponsors a "for good" event or resource.
- For my platforms: show learning outcomes, not just workload savings; make AI decisions visible (why an answer was marked).

## Counterpoint
Harmer (comment): the evidence gap is real, but AI helps in practice; train critically aware students. Kerr: critical-thinking training has little evidence outside narrow domains.
