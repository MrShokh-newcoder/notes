> Siegel's four adolescent trends (emotional intensity, social engagement, novelty-seeking, creative exploration) can be designed into lessons instead of being managed as behaviour problems.

## Moves
- Explain the *why* of each task; tie it to students' lives; offer topic choice.
- Group roles like professional teams; student-led icebreakers.
- Fewer worksheets; choice boards and game elements for novelty.
- Real-world problem projects with alternative outputs (prototype, podcast, app).
- Reflection on feelings as well as content.

## Sources
Siegel, *Brainstorm* (popular book); Sousa, *How the Brain Learns*.

## Limits
Popular neuroscience ("dopamine"). Treat it as a design checklist, not evidence that achievement rises. Ages 12–24.
