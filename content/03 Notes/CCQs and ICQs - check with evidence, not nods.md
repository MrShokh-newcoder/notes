> Students nod to save face, so 'Does everyone understand?' tells you nothing. CCQs check meaning; ICQs check task set-up. Both get short, observable answers. 'Checking questions turn “they nodded” into “they showed me.”'

## Writing CCQs
1. Break the concept into 2–4 components ("I've lost my keys" → past action, time unknown, still relevant now).
2. One question per component with a one- or two-word answer: "Did it happen before now? Do we know when? Does he have his keys now?"
3. Don't use the target form in the question; no "why" questions needing metalanguage.
4. Skip transparent items (cognates, page numbers).

## ICQs
2–3 questions: alone or in pairs? how many? how long?

## Limits
No evidence cited; over-checking wastes time; a badly targeted CCQ tests the wrong thing.
