> "Whenever we open a bracket, we need to close it." Closings are usually what gets cut when a task overruns, yet they recycle language and give the teacher data for the next lesson.

## Favourites from the 21
- **3-2-1 exit ticket** (3 things learned, 2 questions, 1 use).
- **Choose a word:** each student writes a sentence with a studied word on a sticky note; the teacher reuses them as next lesson's gap-fill.
- **Inside-outside circle:** each student writes a question about the lesson, asks it, rotates.
- **Error auction:** 6–8 sentences, some wrong; groups bid from an imaginary 100.
- Exit quiz (Kahoot, Forms); Jeopardy or noughts and crosses only if ~10 minutes remain.

## Limits
A menu, not tested; no levels.

## Why it matters *(Claude)*
A closing is the cheapest retrieval practice slot in the lesson, and the "choose a word" routine builds next-lesson spacing automatically. Protect it by timing the closing first when planning, and cutting from the middle.
