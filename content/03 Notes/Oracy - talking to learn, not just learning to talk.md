> Oracy gives speaking and listening equal weight with literacy and numeracy. In children's ELT, 'talking to learn' (talk as a vehicle for thinking) matters as much as 'learning to talk'. Ten self-audit questions check whether classroom talk does this.

## Self-audit (from the post's questions)
1. Record yourself: who talks most? (the "two-thirds rule": teachers talk ~2/3 of the time).
2. Count closed vs open, higher-order questions.
3. Track IRF and AA (child asks, teacher answers) patterns; vary feedback so it isn't ritual.
4. Wait time: pause, neutral body language.
5. Model thinking and useful language; monitor oracy via recordings, conferencing, learner diaries.

## Theory
Vygotskian principles; "two-thirds rule" research (US, 1970s, Flanders *(Claude)*).
