> Movement is worthwhile only when it serves a clear educational purpose, such as linking words to meanings, spelling, sequencing or attention. VAK learning styles lack support. Give every child multisensory experience instead.

## Claim
Fleming & Mills's (1992) VAK has "no empirical evidence that children ever use only one sensory approach to learning." Gardner himself criticised "kids crawling along the floor" as not bodily-kinaesthetic intelligence.

## Purposeful activities
- **Three bears**: big/small/tiny actions for relative size.
- **Spelling gym**: visualise a word, act each letter's position relative to the line (ascenders/descenders).
- **Physical line-up**: children hold word cards and link arms to form contractions (*there + is → there's*).
- Instructions to make a paper model; action-sequenced story retelling; finger rhymes; short refocusing bursts.

## Limits
Stressful with exuberant classes. Ask: does this movement move the *learning* forward?
