> Lewis: language is grammaticalised lexis, not lexicalised grammar. Thornbury (1998) said it was an idea without a methodology; by 2010, usage-based theory and corpora had moved him towards Lewis.

## Claim
Lewis (1993, p. 34): "language consists of grammaticalized lexis, not lexicalized grammar". Thornbury's 1998 critique: no syllabus guidance, eclectic chunk-awareness activities, risk of **premature lexicalisation** — "all chunks, no pineapple" (lots of retrievable lexis, no generative grammar). In 2010 he is "more inclined to Michael's position" because corpus linguistics and usage-based theory supply the missing rationale.

## Mechanism / theory
- Corpus linguistics / COBUILD (Sinclair); Hoey's lexical priming (comments); Simpson-Vlach & Ellis (2010) on academic formulas.
- Richards & Rodgers (2001, p. 138): "still an idea in search of an approach and a methodology."
- Skehan's lexical → syntactic → relexicalisation cycle (comment) explains the fossilisation worry.
- Lewis's "frequency fallacy": frequency ≠ utility.

## Classroom moves
- Highlight multi-word chunks and collocations as units to notice and reuse.
- Combine a pre-selected lexical syllabus (frequent, pragmatically useful formulae) with emergent language.
- Teach chunks from texts/transcripts, not decontextualised lists.
- Reformulate learner output at the point of need.

## Limits & critique
- Selection problem: "where do you begin?" (Pickles, comment).
- Dave Willis's lexical syllabus (1990) predates Lewis (Peppard, comment).
- Lewis himself later disowned "lexis is the answer".
