> Errors mostly come from items learners have met too few times to know their co-text, not from missing rules. Correction is just more priming, so 'prevention is infinitely desirable to cure'.

## Claim
Learners use new items creatively from thin priming, often an L1 prime. The task itself often *creates* the errors by demanding language learners haven't met enough.

## Procedures
- **Weekly chunk dictation** (Andrew Walkley's practice): record ~50 chunks or collocations (Vocaroo), send the link; students write them down as a dictation, then write sentences showing how they'd use or hear each one. The teacher reformulates by email.
- In class, **reformulate on the board**: rework learners' sentences into natural English without a rule lecture.
- Treat grammar correction as "a condensed and more focused part of the priming process".

## Limits
Correction won't produce correct use immediately. Error sources vary with L1 and reading exposure. No study cited.
