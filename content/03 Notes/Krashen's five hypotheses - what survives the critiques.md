> A clear DELTA-revision summary of Krashen's Monitor Model (Input, Acquisition–Learning, Monitor, Natural Order, Affective Filter) and its standard critiques.

## The five hypotheses
1. **Acquisition–learning:** subconscious acquisition vs conscious learning.
2. **Monitor:** learned knowledge only edits output, given time, focus on form and knowledge of the rule.
3. **Natural order:** grammatical morphemes are acquired in a predictable order.
4. **Input (i+1):** acquisition comes from comprehensible input slightly beyond current level.
5. **Affective filter:** anxiety and low motivation block input.

## Standard critiques
Untestable constructs (McLaughlin 1987, *Claude*); the acquisition/learning split is fuzzy; output matters (Swain); natural-order evidence rests on morpheme studies; explicit instruction speeds learning (Norris & Ortega 2000, *Claude*).

## What survives *(Claude)*
Input is necessary; order effects are real (see Pienemann); affect modulates engagement. What doesn't: "no interface" and input-only sufficiency.

## Watch out
The page's FAQ lists "Output Hypothesis" as one of Krashen's five, which is wrong (it's Swain's response).
