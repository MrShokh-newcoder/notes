> Revisit material when it is 'still familiar but not fresh'. In UCLA physics, students who did mixed (interleaved) homework outscored the sequential group by 74% on a transfer exam. There is 'no universal ideal spacing schedule'.

## Research cited
- Samani & Pan (2021) *npj Science of Learning*: interleaved physics homework → +74% on unfamiliar-problem exam.
- Carpenter et al. (2022) *Nature Reviews Psychology*: broad benefits of spacing, but no single ideal gap.
- Dunlosky et al. (2013) *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*: mechanism (retrieval after delay, consolidation, varied context) *(Claude: the article names no authors; this is the standard citation for that DOI)*.

## Moves
- 5-question mini-quizzes mixing this week, last week and older material; light marking, answers reviewed.
- 90-second rapid review at the start of class.
- Spread homework across days and topics; interleave problem types so learners must *choose* a strategy.
- Map where core concepts recur across the term.

## Limits
- US K-12 framing; the citations are as printed in the article and weren't checked against the primary papers.
- College physics ≠ young learners' vocabulary.
