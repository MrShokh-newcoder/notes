> Smith summarises Ellis's (2002) principles as a flexible guide. He himself used 'task-supported' teaching (tasks plus explicit grammar and exam practice) and thinks full TBLT suits intermediate+ learners more than novices.

## The eight principles (paraphrased)
1. Challenging but achievable difficulty, with pre-task support.
2. Clear goals: choose a fluency, accuracy or complexity focus via planning.
3. Explain the task's value for language development.
4. Learners take an active role and negotiate meaning.
5. Encourage risk-taking and pushed output.
6. Primary focus on meaning; a real outcome.
7. Opportunities to focus on form before, during or after.
8. Learners evaluate their performance.

## Caution
The post's summary was drafted with ChatGPT; check Ellis's original paper before citing. *(Claude)* Ellis's widely cited list is "Principles of instructed language learning" (2005, *System* 33) and his task criteria in Ellis (2003); verify which the post means.

## Examples
Pre-teach suggestion frames for a dinner-party planning task; "choose the best holiday" listening task; "Alibi" for past tenses; short reflection only for older learners.
