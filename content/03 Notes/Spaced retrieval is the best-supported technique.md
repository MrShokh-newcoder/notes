> Conti ranks ten techniques by research strength. Spaced retrieval practice comes first, then interaction with corrective feedback, input flood + FonF, pushed output, task repetition, structured input, text reconstruction, chunks, guided revision, metalinguistic tasks.

## The ranking (Conti's own reading of the literature)
1. Spaced retrieval practice ("very strong"): Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Pavlik & Anderson 2005; Karpicke & Blunt 2011
2. Interaction with corrective feedback: Long 1996; Lyster & Ranta 1997; Mackey & Goo 2007
3. Input flood + focus on form: Schmidt 2001; Doughty & Williams 1998
4. Pushed output: Swain 1995, 2005
5. Task repetition with variation: Bygate 2001
6. Structured input: VanPatten & Cadierno 1993
7. Text reconstruction (dictogloss) / sentence processing
8. Lexical chunks: Nation; Ellis 2002
9. Editing and guided revision
10. Metalinguistic awareness tasks

## How to do spaced retrieval (classroom)
- Warm-ups that revisit *earlier weeks* (transformations, oral recall, closed-book dictation).
- Systematic rotation: old items recur alongside new ones.
- Peer quizzing games (Oral Ping-Pong, No Snakes No Ladders, Pyramid Translation).
- Spaced flashcards (Anki); revisit in varied social configurations.
- Key idea: retrieval "recurs after learners have begun to forget."

## Limits & critique
- Self-described as "my own reading and interpretation". Ratings are judgements, not meta-analytic effect sizes.
- Some support is self-citation (Conti 2004 thesis).
- Much of the evidence base is lab-based or vocabulary-specific. *(Claude)*
