> Automaticity is fluent use without extra effort or attention, drawing on memorised procedures. Memorised chunks alone give a fast but limited repertoire; learners need to customise them in activities that are communicative yet formulaic and repetitive.

## Gatbonton & Segalowitz's (1988) five criteria
Activities should be **genuinely communicative**, **psychologically authentic**, **focused**, **formulaic** and **inherently repetitive**.

## Activities that fit
- **Class photo:** learners arrange the group with partly filled frames (*X, stand behind Y*); ~10 minutes before the end for urgency.
- **4/3/2:** same talk to three partners in 4, 3, 2 minutes.
- Guessing games; Find someone who…; grids with *Can you…?*
- Teacher upgrades phrasing at the point of need, mid-task.

## Sources
Gatbonton & Segalowitz (1988) *TESOL Quarterly* 22/3; Segalowitz (2003) in Doughty & Long (eds); Segalowitz (2010) *Cognitive Bases of Second Language Fluency*; Dörnyei (2009).

## Counterargument
Luiz Otávio Barros: learners take the minimum-effort route; unless the target items are *essential* to the task, they won't use them. Hence pre- or post-task language focus.
