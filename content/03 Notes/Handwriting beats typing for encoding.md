> Handwriting engages broader networks and gives better memory and early-literacy outcomes than typing. But typing is an equaliser for slow writers, ELLs and some learners with disabilities, so balance them rather than replace one with the other.

## Evidence cited
- Spanish kindergarten (2025): letter naming 92% (handwriting) vs 76% (typing); word naming 72% vs 38%.
- Grades 5 and 9 story recall: grade 9 recall 26% (paper), 22% (touchscreen), 19% (laptop) (2019).
- Note-taking: Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014) and a 2024 meta-analysis show small advantages (+0.13 SD conceptual, +0.29 SD factual).
- Keyboarding is ~55% faster for teens (2011).

## Moves
- Handwriting for letter formation, early literacy, and note-taking where pace allows.
- Paper retrieval before digital tasks.
- Paraphrase and sketch in notes; don't transcribe verbatim.

## Limits
- US K-12 framing; the citations are as printed in the article and weren't checked against the primary papers.
- Several effects are small; some samples are tiny.
