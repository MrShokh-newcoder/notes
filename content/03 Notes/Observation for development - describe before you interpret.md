> Observation is usually tied to evaluation, which makes it threatening and shuts down reflection. For developmental observation, start feedback with description of what happened, then interpretation, and only then suggestions.

## Before observing, decide
Purpose (development **or** evaluation; don't mix them) · guidelines · mode (live, video, audio) · observer (critical friend, peer, supervisor) · which class · whether the teacher prepares differently.

## Feedback sequence
**Describe → interpret → suggest**, structured by *What? So what? Now what?* (a common simplification of Kolb's experiential cycle).

## Other reflection tools
Audio or video recording, learner surveys, lesson reports, retroactive planning (write the plan of the lesson you actually taught).

## Theory
Richards & Farrell (2005) *Professional Development for Language Teachers* *(Claude: the post gives it as Farrell & Richards 2008, so verify the edition)*. Wajnryb, *Classroom Observation Tasks*; Malderez & Bodóczky, *Mentor Courses*.

## Workshop activities
Agree or disagree with 20 statements about feedback; "10 things Mike doesn't want to hear in feedback sessions".
