> A wrong listening answer can come from sound discrimination, stress, segmentation, word recognition, grammar, speed, memory or integrating meaning. Replay-explain-move-on fixes none of them; diagnose the bottleneck, intervene on it, then check on a new recording.

## Routine
1. Students do the task alone.
2. Replay the extract: what did you think you heard?
3. Transcript: highlight words you *knew* but didn't recognise; mark unknown words differently.
4. Probe one suspected bottleneck: Faulty Echo (sounds), Stress Detective (prosody), Break the Flow / Chunk Tracking (segmentation), Word Grab (recognition), Grammar Detective (grammar), Connector Snap (discourse).
5. Intervene; fade transcript support.
6. Check on a **new** recording, ideally a week later.
- Learner forms: "Listening Detective" (younger), "Why did I miss that?" (older).

## Limits
No controlled studies of the approach; slowing audio distorts prosody; learners' self-explanations aren't always reliable.
