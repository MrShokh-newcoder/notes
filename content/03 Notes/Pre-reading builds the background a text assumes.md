> Teens misread the opening of Maugham's 'Mr Know-All' because they lacked what the text assumes: post-WWI timing, why a sea voyage takes two weeks, San Francisco to Yokohama, what British surnames signal. A video in which the narrator 'chats' with viewers supplies it before reading.

## Pre-reading content
- Time frame (end of WWI) → why the voyage makes sense.
- Map: San Francisco to Yokohama.
- Do surnames like Smith or Brown signal nationality? The British Empire context.
- Then read in the "visual lesson" format: text, audio and images together.

## Context
Naomi Epstein teaches EFL to deaf and hard-of-hearing teens; visual scaffolding is her core method, but the point is general.

## Theory *(Claude)*
Schema theory (Carrell & Eisterhold 1983): comprehension fails less from unknown words than from missing *content* schemata. Pre-teaching background beats pre-teaching vocabulary when the text is culturally distant, which is most literature for Uzbek teens.
