> Bring dry lexical exercises to life: analyse all the language in them, round up with stress and paraphrase, board whole gapped sentences, then run a speaking task and reformulate what students tried to say.

## Part 1: the teacher's moves
1. Before class, study the exercise for *all* the language present, not only the targets.
2. Monitor pair-work; signal errors ("Uh-uh!" + point) without giving answers.
3. Stop at ~60–70% done → pairs compare → whole-class round-up.
4. In the round-up, stress-mark items (a-DDRESS) and **paraphrase to elicit**: "they might MMM street drinking… → BAN".
5. Board **whole gapped sentences** that extend the target items (prepared or improvised).
6. Personal follow-up questions on the same language.

## Part 2: getting students to use it
([source](https://hughdellar.wordpress.com/2013/10/25/ways-of-exploiting-lexical-self-study-material-in-the-classroom-part-two-some-things-we-can-get-students-to-do/))
- A speaking task that needs the new language (e.g. rank three of ten social problems, explain why). Model your own answer first.
- Monitor and note what students *try* to say.
- Board grammaticalised, gapped versions; elicit by paraphrase; then a second pair-change task.
- If you can't reformulate live, pre-plan what students "MIGHT or COULD say".

## Limits
Takes longer than "checking answers"; depends on the teacher's real-time reformulation skill.
