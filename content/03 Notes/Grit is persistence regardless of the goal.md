> Grit repackages self-discipline. Persistence is amoral and sometimes unhealthy, Duckworth's evidence is partly circular, and the grit agenda shifts attention from curriculum to children's compliance.

## Arguments
- West Point: grit predicted not quitting. "What does that prove, other than that people who are persistent persist?"
- Spelling Bee: grit predicted performance; openness to experience predicted *worse* performance.
- Miller & Wrosch: people who can disengage from unattainable goals have better well-being.
- "This would be a better world if people who were up to no good had less grit."

## Instead
Teach *when* to persist and when to quit; choose worthwhile goals first; make the curriculum engaging; give students decisions.

## Counter-evidence *(Claude)*
Credé, Tynan & Harms (2017, *JPSP*) meta-analysis: grit overlaps heavily with conscientiousness and adds little predictive power. That supports Kohn's scepticism empirically.
