> A five-year study of ~3,500 German students: intrinsic motivation and deep learning strategies predicted long-term maths growth; extrinsic motivation and surface (rote) strategies did not; intelligence predicted initial level but not growth.

## The study *(identified by Claude from the DOI in the post)*
Murayama, Pekrun, Lichtenfeld & vom Hofe (2013), "Predicting long-term growth in students' mathematics achievement: The unique contributions of motivation and cognitive strategies", *Child Development* 84(4). Grades 5–10, Bavaria (PALMA project).

## Findings as reported
- Intrinsic motivation, perceived control and deep strategies (elaboration, connecting ideas) → growth.
- Extrinsic motivation and surface strategies → short-term gains at most.
- Perceived control effects were less consistent across grades.

## Limits
One subject, one cohort; correlational. Ferlazzo extends it to learning in general, which the authors don't.
