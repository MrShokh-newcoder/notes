**Highlights / notes file:** Thinking in English - John B. Muciaccia

> Language is learned by copying and participating, not memorising; success breeds success, so learners must see results. Also argues learners need exposure to (American) culture.

## From my highlights
- "He who thinks, learns." Children don't memorise, they copy what they hear and see; copying (intuitive learning) lasts.
- Mastery comes through participation; it takes time to learn and practice to master.
- "In learning a language, success breeds success. The student needs to see results."
- Learning English without exposure to American culture makes the job harder.

## Read critically *(Claude)*
The imitation claim echoes usage-based learning but overstates it: memorised chunks *are* a large part of fluency (see Conti, Dellar). The American-culture claim reflects a native-speaker target model; for most of my learners, English is a lingua franca.
